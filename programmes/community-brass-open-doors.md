## Programme narrative

Windsor Forest Community Brass is a non-competing adult brass band that meets weekly in Thorpe, Surrey. It welcomes people of every ability — from complete beginners to those returning to an instrument after many years — in a deliberately safe, inclusive and sociable setting built around participation and enjoyment rather than performance standard. The band is BandSafe accredited and self-sustains on a modest £10/month subscription.

**Open Doors** turns that welcoming ethos into a funded widening-access and wellbeing project. Many adults who would benefit most — people experiencing isolation or loneliness, older adults, disabled adults, and those on low incomes — do not join community music because of cost (subs plus the price of an instrument), lack of a beginner-friendly entry route, or simply not knowing they would be welcome. Open Doors removes those barriers: a beginner-and-returner "come & try" pathway with a gentle on-ramp into the main band, free or subsidised places for those facing financial barriers, and a pool of loan instruments so no one is excluded by the cost of a cornet.

The project's purpose is social as much as musical: regular, structured, face-to-face contact and shared achievement are a well-recognised route to reduced isolation and improved wellbeing. The band already provides the community and the safeguarding; Open Doors funds the access mechanisms and the light-touch measurement needed to show the difference it makes.

## Impact pathway

Barrier removal (bursary place + loan instrument + beginner-friendly session) → an isolated / low-income / older / disabled adult attends → regular weekly attendance and social connection → improved wellbeing, confidence and sense of belonging → progression from the beginner strand into sustained membership of the main band.

*Not yet evidenced:* the wellbeing and isolation-reduction outcomes are claimed on the strength of the model, not yet on WFBA's own data. A simple, consistent measure (attendance, retention, a short before/after wellbeing question set) is a prerequisite so future applications can evidence rather than assert impact. Current band membership numbers and demand from barrier-facing adults are also not yet documented.

## Sustainability note

The host band is already self-sustaining on member subscriptions and community/paid gigs. Open Doors is designed so grant funding does not become a permanent cost: instruments are a one-off capital purchase that keep serving new joiners; bursary places taper as participants who can afford subs move onto them; and the beginner strand feeds retained, subscription-paying members into the main band. The grant creates a widened, more inclusive participation pathway rather than subsidising ongoing running costs.
